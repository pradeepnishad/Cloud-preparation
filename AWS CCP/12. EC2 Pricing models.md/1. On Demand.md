# 💰 EC2 On-Demand Pricing

## 1. Definition

**On-Demand pricing is a Pay-As-You-Go (PAYG) pricing model.**

You use EC2 compute capacity and **pay for what you consume**, without making a long-term commitment.

```text
Launch EC2
    ↓
Use compute
    ↓
Pay for usage
```

According to your source, when you launch an EC2 instance, **On-Demand pricing is the default pricing option**.

---

## 2. Why use On-Demand?

The main advantages highlighted in the source are:

* **No upfront payment**
* **No long-term commitment**
* Pay based on usage
* Easy to start and stop resources

This makes it useful when you don't know how long you'll need the EC2 instance.

---

## 3. How does it work?

You launch an EC2 instance:

```text
EC2 → On-Demand
```

You are charged based on the applicable billing unit.

Your source states:

* Certain listed Linux/Windows configurations → **per-second billing, minimum 60 seconds**
* Other instance types → **per-hour billing**
* AWS pricing displays an **hourly rate** when looking up EC2 pricing.

So for your exam, the important concept is:

> **On-Demand = pay for the compute capacity you use, without upfront commitment.**

---

## 4. When should you use On-Demand?

Your source specifically highlights workloads that are:

### Short-term

You only need the server temporarily.

Example:

```text
Development
   ↓
Launch EC2
   ↓
Test application
   ↓
Terminate EC2
```

### Spiky

Workload suddenly increases for a short period.

### Unpredictable

You don't know exactly when or how much compute you'll need.

### New applications / experiments

You're developing a new application and don't yet know whether you'll need the infrastructure for weeks, months, or longer.

---

## 5. Scenario

Imagine you're experimenting with a new application.

You need an EC2 instance for testing, but you don't know whether you'll need it for:

* 2 hours
* 2 days
* 2 weeks

Instead of making a long-term commitment, you can use:

**EC2 On-Demand**

```text
Launch
  ↓
Develop
  ↓
Test
  ↓
Terminate
  ↓
Pay for applicable usage
```

---

# 🔥 On-Demand vs Long-Term Commitment

The key idea is:

| On-Demand                              | Commitment-based options                 |
| -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| No long-term commitment                | Commitment involved                      |
| No upfront payment                     | May involve commitment/payment structure |
| Flexible                               | More predictable usage                   |
| Good for short/unpredictable workloads | Better suited to predictable workloads   |

You'll encounter other EC2 pricing options later, such as **Reserved Instances and Savings Plans**, which are designed around commitments and can reduce pricing for eligible usage.
