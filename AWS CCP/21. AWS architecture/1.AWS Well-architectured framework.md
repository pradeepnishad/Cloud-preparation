### AWS Well-Architected Framework — Quick Notes

**What is it?**
The **AWS Well-Architected Framework** is a whitepaper created by AWS to help customers build cloud workloads using AWS-defined **best practices**.

### 🏛️ 5 Pillars

1. **Operational Excellence** — focus on operating and improving workloads effectively.
2. **Security** — protect systems, data, and resources.
3. **Reliability** — ensure workloads can perform correctly and recover from failures.
4. **Performance Efficiency** — use resources efficiently and choose appropriate technologies.
5. **Cost Optimization** — avoid unnecessary costs and maximize value.

### Key points from the slide

* The framework provides different **“lenses”** for evaluating a cloud workload.
* It includes:

  * **General Definitions**
  * **General Design Principles**
  * **Review Process**
* Each pillar has its own **detailed whitepaper**.
* Think of the five pillars as the **foundation supporting your cloud workload**. If the foundation isn't solid, structural problems can arise.

**Easy way to remember:**

> **O-S-R-P-C** → **Operational Excellence, Security, Reliability, Performance Efficiency, Cost Optimization**.

## AWS Well-Architected Framework — General Definitions

### 🏛️ 5 Pillars

| Pillar                     | Meaning                                 |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Operational Excellence** | Run and monitor systems                 |
| **Security**               | Protect data and systems, mitigate risk |
| **Reliability**            | Mitigate and recover from disruptions   |
| **Performance Efficiency** | Use computing resources effectively     |
| **Cost Optimization**      | Get the lowest price                    |

> **Important:** These pillars involve **trade-offs based on business context**. The overall goal is to deliver **business value**.

### 📖 General Definitions

* **Component** → Code, configuration, and AWS resource against a requirement.
* **Workload** → A set of components that work together to deliver business value.
* **Milestones** → Key changes to your architecture throughout the product life cycle.
* **Architecture** → How components work together in a workload.
* **Technology Portfolio** → A collection of workloads required for the business to operate.

### 🧠 Easy way to remember

**Component → Workload → Architecture → Technology Portfolio**

And the 5 pillars:

**O → S → R → P → C**

**Operational Excellence → Security → Reliability → Performance Efficiency → Cost Optimization**

## AWS Well-Architected — On Architecture

### 🏢 On-Premise Enterprise vs ☁️ AWS

The AWS Well-Architected Framework is designed around a **different team structure** from traditional enterprises.

### On-Premise Enterprise — Centralized Teams

Typically has specialized, centralized roles:

* **Technical Architect** → Infrastructure
* **Solution Architect** → Software
* **Data Architect**
* **Networking Architect**
* **Security Architect**

These teams may be managed using frameworks such as **TOGAF** or **Zachman Framework**.

### AWS — Distributed Teams

AWS uses a more **distributed team structure with flexible roles**.

It is built around:

**1. Practices**

* Team experts
* **Raise the Bar**

**2. Mechanisms**

* Automated checks for standards

**3. Amazon Leadership Principles**

AWS teams are also supported by a **virtual community of SMEs (Subject Matter Experts) and Principal Engineers**.

Example from the slide:

* Lunch-and-learn talks
* Knowledge can be reused as onboarding material

### 🧠 Key idea

**Traditional enterprise:**

> Centralized specialized architecture teams

**AWS approach:**

> Distributed teams + flexible roles + practices + mechanisms + leadership principles

The idea is that distributed teams can introduce new risks, and AWS addresses these through **practices, mechanisms, and leadership principles**.

# Amazon Leadership Principles — Detailed Notes

## 1. Customer Obsession 🎯

**Meaning:** Start with the customer and work backward.

Amazon prioritizes understanding what customers actually need rather than simply focusing on competitors or internal convenience.

### Key ideas

* Understand customer needs.
* Build solutions around customer problems.
* Earn and maintain customer trust.
* Think about the customer experience.
* Work backward from the desired customer outcome.

### Example

Instead of saying:

> "We built this feature, so customers should use it."

Think:

> "What problem does the customer have, and what should we build to solve it?"

### Easy memory

**Customer first.**

---

# 2. Ownership 🏠

**Meaning:** Act like an owner rather than someone who is only responsible for their assigned task.

Amazon expects leaders to think about the **long-term health of the business**, not just short-term results.

### Key ideas

* Take responsibility.
* Think long-term.
* Don't say **"that's not my job."**
* Consider the impact on the entire organization.
* Take responsibility for problems instead of passing them to someone else.

Amazon explicitly describes ownership as acting on behalf of the whole company and not sacrificing long-term value for short-term results. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Cloud example

You notice that an EC2 server repeatedly crashes.

A non-owner might say:

> "The server team handles that."

An owner thinks:

> "This is affecting the service. I'll investigate, document the issue, and make sure it gets resolved."

### Easy memory

**Think like you own it.**

---

# 3. Invent and Simplify 💡

**Meaning:** Create better solutions while making things simpler.

Amazon encourages innovation but doesn't want unnecessary complexity.

### Key ideas

* Look for better ways of doing things.
* Automate repetitive work.
* Remove unnecessary steps.
* Don't accept complexity just because "that's how we've always done it."
* Look for ideas outside your own organization.

### DevOps example

Instead of manually:

**Login → Create VM → Configure VM → Install software → Configure monitoring**

create an automated process using:

**Terraform + scripts + CI/CD**

That's both **invention and simplification**.

### Easy memory

**Innovate + simplify.**

---

# 4. Are Right, A Lot 🧠

**Meaning:** Make good decisions consistently, especially when there is uncertainty.

This does **not** mean you must always be correct.

Amazon describes this principle around **judgment, instincts, diverse perspectives, and challenging your own assumptions**. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Key ideas

* Use good judgment.
* Gather information.
* Listen to different opinions.
* Question your assumptions.
* Be willing to change your mind.
* Look for evidence that proves your idea wrong.

### Example

You believe an application is slow because of CPU usage.

Instead of immediately increasing the EC2 instance size, investigate:

* CPU
* Memory
* Network
* Disk I/O
* Application logs
* Database performance

Then make the decision based on evidence.

### Easy memory

**Good judgment, not ego.**

---

# 5. Learn and Be Curious 📚

**Meaning:** Never stop learning.

Amazon expects leaders to continuously improve their knowledge and explore new possibilities. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Key ideas

* Keep learning.
* Ask questions.
* Explore new technologies.
* Improve existing skills.
* Don't assume you already know everything.

### Your AWS example

You're currently learning:

**AWS → Networking → CloudWatch → CloudTrail → DevOps**

This principle fits perfectly.

You don't just learn:

> "What is CloudWatch?"

You also ask:

> "Why is CloudWatch used? How does it work? How would I troubleshoot using it?"

### Easy memory

**Always keep learning.**

---

# 6. Hire and Develop the Best 👥

**Meaning:** Build strong teams and help people become better.

Amazon expects leaders to raise the performance bar through hiring, promotion, coaching, and development. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Key ideas

* Hire talented people.
* Maintain high standards.
* Help employees grow.
* Mentor others.
* Develop future leaders.
* Recognize exceptional talent.

### Example

A senior DevOps engineer shouldn't only solve problems themselves.

They should also teach junior engineers:

> "Here's why the deployment failed, here's how I diagnosed it, and here's how you can troubleshoot it next time."

### Easy memory

**Build great people and teams.**

---

# 7. Insist on the Highest Standards 📈

**Meaning:** Don't accept poor quality as "good enough."

Amazon expects leaders to continually raise the quality bar and fix problems properly. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Key ideas

* Maintain high quality.
* Don't normalize recurring problems.
* Fix root causes.
* Continuously improve standards.
* Prevent defects from moving further through the system.

### DevOps example

Bad approach:

> Server keeps crashing → restart it every time.

Better approach:

> Server keeps crashing → investigate logs → identify root cause → fix root cause → add monitoring/alerting.

### Easy memory

**Don't just fix the symptom. Fix the cause.**

---

# 8. Think Big 🌎

**Meaning:** Don't limit yourself to small improvements when a much larger opportunity exists.

Amazon encourages leaders to create bold directions and think beyond immediate limitations. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Small thinking

> "I'll automate this one server."

### Big thinking

> "Can we automate infrastructure provisioning for the entire organization?"

### Key ideas

* Create ambitious goals.
* Think beyond current limitations.
* Look for large opportunities.
* Create a vision.
* Think about long-term impact.

### Easy memory

**Think beyond the immediate problem.**

---

# 9. Bias for Action ⚡

**Meaning:** Act quickly when appropriate instead of endlessly analyzing.

Amazon recognizes that many decisions are **reversible**, so they don't always require lengthy analysis. The principle emphasizes calculated risk-taking. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Example

You notice a monitoring issue.

Bad approach:

> Spend three weeks discussing whether to create an alert.

Better approach:

> Investigate → create a reasonable alert → test it → improve it.

### Important

**Bias for Action ≠ careless action.**

It means:

> **Move quickly when the decision is reversible and the risk is understood.**

### Easy memory

**Don't wait unnecessarily. Act.**

---

# 10. Frugality 💰

**Meaning:** Accomplish more with fewer resources.

This doesn't simply mean "spend as little money as possible."

It means using resources intelligently. Amazon describes constraints as encouraging resourcefulness, self-sufficiency, and invention. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Cloud example

You need a development environment.

Option A:

> Run expensive EC2 instances 24/7.

Option B:

> Use appropriately sized instances and shut them down when they're not required.

That's **cloud cost optimization through frugality**.

### Key ideas

* Avoid unnecessary spending.
* Use resources efficiently.
* Find creative solutions.
* Don't equate bigger budgets with better results.

### Easy memory

**Do more with less.**

---

# 11. Earn Trust 🤝

**Meaning:** Build trust through honesty, respect, transparency, and accountability.

Amazon describes leaders as attentive listeners who speak candidly, treat others respectfully, and are willing to be self-critical. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Key ideas

* Listen.
* Be honest.
* Communicate clearly.
* Admit mistakes.
* Accept criticism.
* Treat people respectfully.
* Don't hide problems.

### Example

Your deployment caused an outage.

Instead of:

> "The deployment system failed."

Say:

> "I made a configuration mistake during deployment. Here's what happened, what I've fixed, and how we'll prevent it next time."

That's how trust is built.

### Easy memory

**Be honest + reliable + respectful.**

---

# 12. Dive Deep 🔍

**Meaning:** Understand the details instead of relying only on high-level information.

This is especially important for **Cloud and DevOps**.

Amazon describes this as operating at all levels, staying connected to details, auditing frequently, and investigating when metrics and anecdotes don't agree. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Example

Dashboard says:

**CPU = 20%**

But users report the application is slow.

A shallow approach:

> "CPU is fine."

A deep approach:

Check:

* Memory
* Disk I/O
* Network
* Application logs
* Database
* Latency
* CloudWatch metrics
* Recent deployments

### Easy memory

**Don't stop at the dashboard. Investigate.**

---

# 13. Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit 🗣️

**Meaning:** Respectfully disagree when you believe a decision is wrong, but once the decision is made, fully support it.

Amazon expects leaders to challenge decisions when they disagree rather than staying silent just to avoid conflict. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Example

Your team wants to deploy a configuration that you believe will cause an outage.

You should say:

> "I disagree because our testing shows X. I recommend Y."

If the team decides to proceed anyway after discussion:

> "I still disagree, but I've committed to the decision. I'll help make it successful."

### Important distinction

**Disagree → Explain → Decision → Commit**

Not:

**Disagree → Complain → Refuse to help**

### Easy memory

**Speak up, then commit.**

---

# 14. Deliver Results 🎯

**Meaning:** Ultimately, good ideas must produce real outcomes.

Amazon describes this as focusing on important inputs and delivering with the right quality and on time, even despite setbacks. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Example

You planned to automate 50 servers.

You spent two months designing the perfect architecture but automated nothing.

That's not successful delivery.

Another engineer automated 20 servers reliably and reduced deployment time by 60%.

That's **delivering results**.

### Key ideas

* Focus on outcomes.
* Meet deadlines.
* Maintain quality.
* Overcome setbacks.
* Don't confuse activity with achievement.

### Easy memory

**Results matter.**

---

# 15. Strive to be Earth's Best Employer 🌱

**Meaning:** Create an environment where employees can work safely, grow, and succeed.

Amazon describes this around a safer, more productive, high-performing, diverse and fair workplace, with empathy and employee development. ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Key ideas

* Support employees.
* Encourage growth.
* Empower people.
* Lead with empathy.
* Create a safe workplace.
* Help people prepare for their future.

### Example

A good manager doesn't only ask:

> "Did you finish the ticket?"

They also ask:

> "What skills do you want to develop?"

### Easy memory

**Make people better, safer, and more successful.**

---

# 16. Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility 🌍

**Meaning:** The bigger an organization becomes, the greater its responsibility to consider its impact.

Amazon emphasizes thinking about the effects of its actions on:

* Customers
* Employees
* Partners
* Communities
* The planet
* Future generations ([Amazon.jobs][1])

### Example

A cloud company operates massive data centers.

It should think beyond:

> "Does this make money?"

It should also consider:

* Energy consumption
* Environmental impact
* Security
* Privacy
* Community impact
* Long-term consequences

### Easy memory

**More power → more responsibility.**
