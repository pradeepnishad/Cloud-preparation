This slide is about the **AWS Technical Account Manager (TAM)** — one of the most important features associated with **Enterprise-level AWS support**.

## 👨‍💼 What is a TAM?

A **Technical Account Manager** is essentially a dedicated AWS technical advisor who works closely with an organization.

The key phrase on your slide is:

> **Proactive guidance + reactive support**

### Proactive = before something goes wrong

A TAM helps the customer:

* Plan AWS architecture
* Identify risks
* Improve reliability
* Optimize costs
* Prepare for launches
* Plan migrations
* Conduct workshops
* Follow AWS best practices

### Reactive = when something goes wrong

For example:

```text
Production outage
       ↓
Customer contacts AWS
       ↓
TAM helps coordinate
       ↓
AWS technical teams
       ↓
Problem resolution
```

So a TAM isn't just someone you call **after an incident**. They work with you **before, during, and after** major AWS activities.

---

## 🔹 What does a TAM actually do?

The slide gives several examples.

### 1. Technical guidance

They help customers make technical decisions.

For example:

> "We're migrating 500 servers to AWS. How should we design this environment?"

The TAM can help guide the discussion and connect the customer with the appropriate AWS specialists.

---

### 2. Improve operational health

The TAM helps ensure the customer's AWS environment remains:

* Reliable
* Secure
* Efficient
* Cost-effective

For example, they might identify unnecessary resources or architectural risks.

---

### 3. Incident and risk management

Suppose a company's production application has a serious outage.

The TAM can help coordinate technical discussions around:

* What happened?
* What's the impact?
* What are the risks?
* What should be changed?
* How can the issue be prevented?

---

### 4. Work with different AWS teams

A TAM doesn't work alone.

They may collaborate with:

* AWS Solutions Architects
* Business Developers
* Professional Services
* Sales Account Managers
* AWS Support engineers

So they're somewhat of a **technical bridge between the customer and AWS**.

---

### 5. Migration assistance

This is especially relevant to what you've been studying.

If a large company is migrating:

```text
On-premises
     ↓
AWS
```

A TAM can help with the **planning, technical discussions, risks, and operational readiness** around that migration.

---

## 🔥 Proactive vs Reactive — remember this

This is probably the most important concept from the slide.

### Proactive

> "Let's prevent problems before they happen."

Examples:

* Architecture reviews
* Migration planning
* Cost optimization
* Workshops
* Launch planning
* Risk assessment

### Reactive

> "Something happened. Let's help fix/manage it."

Examples:

* Production incidents
* Service disruptions
* Troubleshooting
* Incident reviews

---

# 🏢 Who needs a TAM?

Think **large enterprise customers**.

For example:

> A bank runs hundreds of applications on AWS and has business-critical workloads.

They don't just want documentation or a support ticket system.

They want someone at AWS who understands their environment and can help them **plan, operate, and optimize AWS at scale**.

That's where a TAM comes in.
