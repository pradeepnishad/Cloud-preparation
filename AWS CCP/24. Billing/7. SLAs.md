# AWS Service Level Agreements (SLA)

### 1. What is an SLA?

**SLA = Service Level Agreement**

It is a **formal commitment between a service provider and a customer** about the expected level of service.

For AWS, this usually means a promised level of **availability/uptime**.

Example from your photo:

> **99.99% availability over a period of 3 months**

If AWS doesn't meet the SLA and the customer satisfies the required conditions, the customer may be eligible for a **Service Credit**.

### 2. SLA vs SLI vs SLO

This is important for the exam:

| Term    | Meaning                           | Easy way to remember |
| ------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **SLA** | Formal commitment                 | **Agreement**        |
| **SLI** | Measurement of actual performance | **Indicator**        |
| **SLO** | Target that should be achieved    | **Objective**        |

Example:

**SLA:** AWS promises a certain availability level.
**SLI:** Actual availability measured = 99.97%.
**SLO:** Target availability = 99.99%.

# 4. Service Credits

If AWS doesn't meet the applicable SLA, the customer may receive **service credits**.

Think:

**AWS misses SLA → Customer may qualify → Service Credit**

It's **not simply an automatic cash refund**.

### Typical exam question

**Q: What is an SLA?**

A. A measurement of actual performance
B. A target performance objective
C. A formal commitment between provider and customer
D. A monitoring service

✅ **Answer: C — SLA is a formal commitment.**

**Q: What happens if AWS fails to meet an applicable SLA?**

✅ The customer **may be eligible for service credits**, subject to the specific SLA terms.
