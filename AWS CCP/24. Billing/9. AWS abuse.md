## AWS Abuse — Simple Explanation

The image says **AWS Trust & Safety** is the team that deals with **abuse occurring on the AWS platform**.

Think of it as:

> **AWS Abuse = reporting misuse of AWS resources.**

### Types of abuse shown in your slide

**1. Spam 📧**
AWS-owned IP addresses are being used to:

* Send unwanted emails
* Spam websites or forums

**2. Port Scanning 🔎**
An AWS-owned IP is sending packets to **multiple ports** on your server to discover potentially unsecured ports.

**3. Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks 🚨**
AWS resources/IPs are being used to **flood your resources with packets**, potentially overwhelming or crashing your server/software.

**4. Intrusion Attempts 🔐**
AWS-owned IP addresses are being used to **attempt to log into your resources**.

**5. Hosting Prohibited Content 🚫**
AWS resources are being used to host or distribute prohibited content, such as:

* Illegal content
* Copyrighted content without permission

**6. Distributing Malware 🦠**
AWS resources are being used to distribute software intentionally created to **compromise or harm computers/machines**.

---

### ⭐ VERY IMPORTANT FOR EXAM

The slide specifically says:

> **AWS Support does NOT deal with Abuse tickets.**

For abuse issues, you need to contact **AWS Abuse** through the abuse contact/reporting process shown in the slide.

### 🧠 Remember it like this:

**Spam → Port Scanning → DoS → Intrusion → Prohibited Content → Malware**

And the key distinction:

**Normal AWS problem → AWS Support**
**Abuse/misuse of AWS → AWS Trust & Safety / Abuse reporting**

**Exam-style question:**

> You notice an AWS-owned IP address is being used to launch a DoS attack against your server. Who should you contact?

✅ **AWS Abuse / Trust & Safety — NOT normal AWS Support.**
